Tahoe Basin Field Guide

Plants and animals of Lake Tahoe and its watershed, from the lake floor to the summit of Freel Peak. Each entry says where it lives, whether it belongs here, and what to know about it.

What is changing

The food web was rebuilt by introductions

Lake trout arrived in the late 1880s, kokanee in 1944, and Mysis shrimp in 1963 to 1965. Mysis ate the native Daphnia nearly to nothing by about 1970, which removed the lake's main algae grazer. UC Davis measured average clarity of 102.4 ft in 1968 and 62.3 ft in 2024.

Cutthroat are coming back

Lahontan cutthroat trout were gone from the lake by the late 1930s. State and federal hatcheries now stock about 100,000 a year, and in 2024 biologists watched fish attempt to spawn on their own in Third Creek at Incline Village, the first such attempt in nearly 90 years.

Mussels are the threat being held off

Quagga, zebra and golden mussels are not in Tahoe. Golden mussels reached the Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta in 2024, and inspectors intercepted 10 boats carrying them out of about 5,200 inspections in the 2026 season. New Zealand mudsnails did get in and were found off the south shore in September 2023.

The forest is second growth under stress

Most of the basin was logged between the 1860s and 1890s to timber the Comstock mines. The forest that grew back is dense and heavy with white fir. Drought, bark beetles and fire now drive it: the Angora Fire in 2007 and the Caldor Fire in 2021 both burned inside the basin.

One plant tracks the waterline

Tahoe yellow cress grows only on Tahoe's sandy beaches, so high lake levels drown its habitat and low levels expose more. The 2025 survey counted about 5,575 plants on 19 beaches.

Scope and limits. This is a selection, not a census. More than a thousand plant species and roughly 300 bird species have been recorded in the basin (both figures approximate). Entries cover what is common, ecologically important, protected, or invasive. Introduction dates and survey numbers come from the sources below; natural history notes are standard Sierra Nevada references. Items where the record is unsettled say so in the entry. Legal listings change, so confirm status with the agency before relying on it.
